Naming the tab, and what your friends see

Two decisions. First what to call the view — "Free time" describes the green space, not the thing itself. Then what a shared link should show, now that there are two ways to look at a trip.

01

What to call the tab

It sits next to "Itinerary", so the pair has to read as two lenses on one trip. Your suggestion is in there as B.

Amy pick
Daily
Reads as "the day-by-day". Short, sits cleanly beside Itinerary, and doesn't over-claim — the view is about hours in a day, not scheduling.
ItineraryDaily
Against: slightly vague on its own — you learn what it means by clicking once.
B
Timetable
Your suggestion. Concrete and instantly understood — everyone knows what a timetable is, and it matches the grid's shape exactly.
ItineraryTimetable
Against: carries a whiff of trains and school; it implies a fixed schedule when the point is the gaps. Also the longest of the three.
C
Hours
The most literal: this is the view where a trip is measured in hours rather than days. Shortest, and pairs oddly well with Itinerary's day-level framing.
ItineraryHours
Against: a bit bare, and "hours" can read as opening hours.
Why A: "Daily" names the unit the view works in, which stays true whether a day is packed or empty — where "Free time" only describes the good case, and reads a little odd on a day that's completely booked. Timetable is the safest for a stranger to understand, so if this view ever gets shared widely, B is the stronger choice.

One privacy call I'd like you to make. The daily card is more revealing than the itinerary one — it shows where you are hour by hour, every night you're out, and (through the daylight shading) which city you're in each day. That's fine for close friends and family, which is who you said this is for.

So the proposal deliberately drops three things from the daily card that the itinerary card shows: hotel names, prices, and confirmation details. A friend sees "you're free Friday afternoon", not "you're at the Hyatt on points". Say the word if you'd rather it showed everything.
