Why B: your question is "how much is open", which is a question about quantity — and a filled bar answers quantity faster than a grid you have to read cell by cell. It also survives 375px, where the week grid (A) needs horizontal scrolling. And unlike C, you still see where in the day the free block sits, which is what decides whether it fits a museum or just a coffee.

A is the right pick if you want to actually schedule to the half-hour. C is the cheapest and reads well on a phone, but "6h 30m free" without a position doesn't tell you if it's morning or evening.

The meal-window question — your call

ApproachWhat it means
Generic windows suggestedShade a standard lunch (12:00–1:30) and dinner (7:00–8:30) on every day, clearly styled as a placeholder, not a booking. Toggleable, and never written to your data. Nothing is invented — it's a visual guide only.
Only real bookingsShow meals only when you've actually booked a restaurant. Most honest, but then every day looks wide open and you'll mentally re-subtract meals each time.
Ask per tripLet you set the meal windows once per trip (some trips you eat at 9pm). Most accurate, one more thing to configure.
Note: I've drawn the meal windows in the mock as hatched grey precisely so they never read as real reservations. Whichever you pick, they'd stay a display-only overlay — no fake bookings written into your data.
03

Where it goes in the app

The chapter view you approved is the trip's "shape". This is a different question — the day's shape — so it wants its own surface rather than replacing what's there.

Placement 1recommended
A toggle on the trip
Two small tabs on the trip header — Itinerary (the chapters) and Free time (the bars). Same trip, two lenses, no new navigation.
Placement 2
Below the chapters
A collapsible "Free time" panel under the itinerary, like the density strip. Always discoverable, but makes an already-long trip page longer.
Placement 3
In the sidebar
A compact version in the desktop sidebar next to the photo. Nice on desktop, but there's no sidebar on mobile — so it would be desktop-only.
